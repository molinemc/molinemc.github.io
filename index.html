Knowing what the model does not cover

5 systems

An agent that cannot tell the difference between a world it understands and one it doesn't will fail confidently. Most of my work starts here.

DiscoverHistory

2008–2017

Reasons backward from partial observations to what must have happened, over mixed discrete and continuous data. Instead of detecting only that a plan failed, the agent can say which unobserved event explains it. DHAgent puts this to work: belief management sits between monitoring and planning, so the agent plans against what it has inferred rather than only what it has seen.

As accurate as a state-of-the-art deductive method while cutting execution time by 95% in the squad-member domain. DHAgent achieved 20–63% more goals than a replanning agent that does not explain, across two hazard domains.
DiscoverHistory: Understanding the Past in Planning and Execution, AAMAS 2012PDF

EML

Explanation-Based Model Learner
2017

Turns explanations of what must have happened into corrections of the agent's own model of its environment. An agent that meets a dynamic nobody told it about stops being repeatedly surprised by it and starts predicting it.

The first general solution to hypothesis-based model learning, reaching 90% of the achievable performance improvement within five learning trials in two domains.
Understanding What May Have Happened in Dynamic, Partially Observable Environments, George Mason University, 2017

ARTUE

Autonomous Response to Unexpected Events
2009–2013

A domain-independent agent that decides which goal to pursue when something surprising happens. It explains the discrepancy first, then forms a new goal out of the explanation, rather than replanning toward a goal that may no longer make sense.

Outperformed both offline planning and discrepancy-based replanning. Independent groups still analyze the architecture and build alternatives against it.
Goal-Driven Autonomy in a Navy Strategy Simulation, AAAI 2010 127 citations

Environment transformation framework

2018–2026

Deliberately transforms an environment to measure how far an agent degrades, and characterizes the classes of novelty an agent actually meets once deployed. The transformations are defined from the environment's side rather than the agent's, which is what makes the measurement comparable: most definitions call a thing novel when it differs from what some particular agent has seen, so two agents can never face the same novelty unless they have lived the same life. Eight dimensions describe how changes differ — whether the agent steers itself into the change or is dragged in, whether it is noticed before or after it starts to hurt, whether noticing takes deliberate effort.

Also fixes the minimum conditions under which a change is worth testing at all: it has to affect performance, be possible to notice, and leave the agent able to do something about it. Without all three there is nothing to learn and the test measures nothing.
An Environment Transformation-based Framework for Comparison of Open-World Learning Agents, AAAI Spring Symposium 2022

Coverage reporting for browsing agents

2026

An evaluation agent that reports the boundary of its own evidence: widgets it could not operate, pages it could read but not enter, actions it never reached. The findings it does produce are the ones it can stand behind.

Repairing plans when conditions change mid-execution

3 systems

When a mission breaks partway through, repair the committed plan instead of restarting it. The same machinery carried across maritime survey, logistics, and undersea operations.

CARPE / AMORE

Cognitive Architecture for RePlanning and Execution
2013–2017

Plans and revises maritime survey and clearance operations, holding a long-horizon plan together as conditions, assets and objectives shift underneath it. Rebuilt three years later as AMORE, a second version under a second contract, whose construction and evaluation I managed.

The approach outlasted the contract that produced it, and outlasted me: the Navy funded a second generation rather than a follow-on study, and the work carried on after I left in 2018.
Minimally Disruptive Schedule Repair for MCM Missions, Proc. SPIE 9454, 2015PDF

ADRUS

Anomaly Detection and Recovery for Unmanned Systems
2014–2015

Detects when something has gone wrong on an unmanned vehicle mid-mission and replans to recover, so a person supervises rather than drives. The problem it was built for is labour, not autonomy for its own sake: unmanned systems of the period ran teleoperated, several operators to a vehicle, which caps how many you can field at all. Moving routine anomaly handling onto the vehicle inverts that ratio.

Demonstrated recovery from unexpected events in simulated logistics and resupply missions, and carried through to a Phase II development contract.
ONR/OSD SBIR Phase II, contract N00014-14-C-0279 — no public report; SBIR data rights

Working alongside people rather than replacing them

3 systems

Agents that model what a particular person is trying to do, instead of averaging everyone into one user. Applied to clinicians, teammates, and customers.

TAD

Trustworthy Algorithmic Delegate
2023–2026

Makes medical triage decisions that match a specific clinician's judgment rather than a population average, combining case-based reasoning, Monte Carlo simulation, and Bayesian diagnosis. Built as principal investigator on DARPA's In the Moment program, in a domain where experts disagree and there is often no correct answer to match against.

Met or exceeded targets in all four independent evaluations, on schedule, outperforming the competing generative-AI team in every round with a system that does not generate.
Aligning to Human Decision-Makers in Military Medical Triage, ICCBR 2024 Algorithmic Decision-Making in Difficult Scenarios, AAAI Spring Symposium 2024 (open PDF)

ASM

Autonomous Squad Member
2015

An agent that accompanies a team on a mission, works out what the team has switched to doing when something goes wrong, and picks its own goal to match. Five components in one decision cycle: it parses what teammates say out loud, explains what must have happened to make that utterance true, recognizes the plan the team has adopted, selects a goal that fits, and plans for it. The explanation step is DiscoverHistory, running inside.

Demonstrated end to end on a reconnaissance scenario: the team is interrupted by a sniper, abandons the mission goal for cover and counter-fire, announces the threat is neutralized, and resumes. The agent tracks all of it from observation and speech alone.
Goal Reasoning for an Autonomous Squad Member, ACS Goal Reasoning Workshop 2015

Synthetic-cohort site evaluation

2026

Walks a website as ten different customers, each with their own goal, budget, and patience, and reports where each one gave up. A retiree who wants a phone number and a procurement officer who wants a spec sheet fail for different reasons, and the report says so instead of averaging them into a bounce rate.

Run across 50 sites to test generality; every report reviewed for understandability and fidelity to real user experience.

Infrastructure other people built on

4 systems

SHOP2‑PDDL+

HTN planner with nonlinear continuous effects
2010

The first hierarchical task network planner able to reason about nonlinear continuous effects. A wait action and a state-projection algorithm let it ask what the world will look like at an arbitrary future moment, with fuel draining, vessels moving, and events firing the instant a threshold is crossed. ARTUE plans with it.

Solved all twenty problems in the rover benchmark; COLIN, the linear-continuous planner it was measured against, failed four. In a Navy training simulation it produced fifty-action plans in about a second across scenarios with a dozen moving vessels.
Planning in Dynamic Environments: Extending HTNs with Nonlinear Continuous Effects, AAAI 2010 PDF

TIELT / LIET

Testbed for Integrating and Evaluating Learning Techniques
2003–2009

The integration layer for a DARPA program: one interface that let learning agents written by five to ten independent teams run against simulated environments none of them had built, including a commercial game engine from the studio that became Rockstar New England. I owned the abstraction and the documentation those teams built against.

Working as intended: a later CaT experiment was set up by adapting the earlier one inside TIELT rather than rebuilding the integration. Rebuilt smaller as LIET, which handled the traffic between agent and simulator, ran the experiment protocol and collected results.
TIELT: A Testbed for Gaming Environments, AAAI 2005

CaT

Case-based Tactician
2005

Learns to beat an opponent it has never seen, in a real-time strategy game, by retrieving the tactic that worked in a similar position and adapting it. A case is a building state, a description of the board, a tactic, and how well that tactic has done; roughly eight decisions decide a five-minute game.

The first case-based system to learn to defeat an unknown real-time strategy opponent. It matched or beat the best hand-evolved counter-strategy on both victory frequency and score — no single static strategy managed both — though it did not significantly beat every counter-strategy individually.
Learning to Win: Case-Based Plan Selection in a Real-Time Strategy Game, IJCAI 2005 workshop ICCBR 2005 version

Casey / CBQL-PR

Case-Based Q-Lambda with Plan Recognition
2009

Learns to play quarterback against a defense that secretly picks one of five plays each down. Rather than learn over the raw movements of eight defenders, it clusters what the defense is doing into a recognized plan and learns over that instead, collapsing a state space of 4.3 billion into two features.

Clustering matched the actual defensive plays more than 99% of the time within a thousand examples, and perfectly in the limit. The recognizing agent reached its best competitor's peak performance in a tenth of the time, and beat both a raw-feature and a hand-optimized baseline on regret and asymptotic advantage (p<.0001 in every comparison).
Beating the Defense: Using Plan Recognition to Inform Learning Agents, FLAIRS 2009
